abstract: "Data re-uploading enhances the learnable function space of parameterized quantum circuits and has become standard practice in circuit-based Quantum Machine Learning (QML). Its role in quantum reservoir computing, however, remains largely unexplored. Here, we introduce data re-uploading into physical quantum reservoir computing and show that repeated input encoding significantly enhances the learnability of higher-order nonlinear polynomials and improves performance on chaotic time-series prediction tasks. We consider protocols with either fixed or increasing computational cost and compare them with a complementary dissipative protocol to distinguish the effects of repeated input encoding from those of additional state overwriting. Using the information processing capacity framework, we identify a memory--nonlinearity trade-off: increasing the number of encodings shifts capacity toward higher-order nonlinearities. In contrast, the dissipative protocol does not extend the range of learnable nonlinear orders and yields only limited improvements in task performance. These results establish data re-uploading as an effective means of tuning and enhancing nonlinear processing in physical quantum reservoir computing."
